Forecasting airport energy demand within 1.7% of actuals

Vancouver International Airport (YVR) · Time series forecasting

ARIMABox-CoxREnergy

1.7% MAPE on holdout data

Problem. Airport operations need reliable energy forecasts to negotiate procurement contracts and plan demand, working from 14 years of monthly consumption data with strong yearly seasonality.

Approach. Stabilized variance with a Box-Cox transformation, fitted a seasonal ARIMA(1,1,0)(0,1,1)[12], and validated on a holdout window of the most recent observations.

Outcome. Forecasts landed within 1.7% of actuals, and 3-year forward projections now support procurement contract and demand planning decisions.

Cutting unnecessary lab tests by 30% without missing patients

Diabetes risk screening · Two-stage machine learning

scikit-learnClassificationHealthcarePython

-30% lab tests at 95% detection sensitivity

Problem. Laboratory testing for diabetes is expensive and slow. The goal: screen patients with non-invasive demographic and physiological data before committing to a lab test.

Approach. A two-stage pipeline: logistic regression for initial risk screening (F1 = 0.78), then a random forest for final classification (F1 = 0.87), with decision thresholds tuned for sensitivity.

Outcome. The pipeline removes 30% of unnecessary lab tests while keeping 95% detection sensitivity, freeing healthcare resources without missing at-risk patients.

Reassigning 2,000 students at minimum cost after a school closure

School bus routing · Mixed-integer linear programming

GurobiOptimizationOperations researchPython

$1.73M/yr optimal plan using 29 buses

Problem. A school closure displaced 2,000 students. The reassignment had to respect school capacities, keep every grade's share between 30% and 36% per school, and cap travel distance, at minimum bus cost.

Approach. Formulated the assignment as a MILP with binary decision variables and solved it with Gurobi, then re-solved under seasonal weather constraints as a scenario analysis.

Outcome. The base plan costs $1,728,000 per year with 29 buses; the weather-constrained plan ($2,112,500) puts a hard number on the safety vs. efficiency trade-off for decision makers.
